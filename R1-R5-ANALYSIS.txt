R1: DECISION TYPES — AI at Phase 0 vs. MECHANICAL at Phase 1
==============================================================

5 DECISIONS AI-JUDGED at Phase 0 → MECHANICAL at Phase 1:

1. GAP TYPE DETECTION
   Why AI works Phase 0: Narrative reading, holistic categorization
   Why mechanical needed Phase 1: 500K records, 100 concurrent users.
     Ambiguity becomes admission gate. Tier-2 mis-categorization cascades across tenants.
     Solution: Mechanical classifier (rules-based, field-structural). AI judgment → drift.

2. CORESPINE SEGMENT RELEVANCE (does this pipeline need this segment?)
   Why AI works Phase 0: Reads goal definition, infers what's needed
   Why mechanical needed Phase 1: Multi-tenant. Vocabulary is TENANT-SPECIFIC.
     AI inference on tenant-specific terms becomes silent drift across deployments.
     Solution: declare_dependency(segment_id) with ratified segment ID.

3. INHERITANCE COMPLETENESS (what items must flow to next stage?)
   Why AI works Phase 0: Traces narrative chain, identifies missing items
   Why mechanical needed Phase 1: At 100 concurrent users, inheritance chains are TENANT-CONTEXT-DEPENDENT.
     Tenant A's "approval" state ≠ Tenant B's. O(N²) audit burden on AI.
     Solution: required_fields[] in stage backpack (hardwired).

4. WIRING STATE UPGRADE READINESS (can element go from Defined→Wired?)
   Why AI works Phase 0: Reads code/tests, judges readiness
   Why mechanical needed Phase 1: Tier Engine permission gates on wiring state (0230).
     200+ rules × multi-tenant = AI upgrade decisions become audit nightmare.
     Solution: Pass 3 gates (1030). Wiring state change triggers auto-propagation.

5. KNOWN CONSTRAINT VALIDITY (is this gap acceptable or critical?)
   Why AI works Phase 0: Reads impact, judges if deferral is safe
   Why mechanical needed Phase 1: Multi-Governor. Governor A accepts constraint, Governor B rejects (different risk profiles).
     Solution: CS-GOV-005 (QA Level matrix). Constraint escalates to correct Governor tier by rule.

---

5 DECISIONS MECHANICAL at Phase 0 → NEED AI LATITUDE at Phase 1:

1. RATIFICATION CEREMONY SEQUENCE (order of approvals)
   Why mechanical Phase 0: 6 steps, one Governor, zero contention
   Why AI needed Phase 1: Multi-Governor (3+). Ceremonies now concurrent and interdependent.
     Mechanical sequence becomes deadlock. AI must coordinate ceremony timing, resolve bottlenecks,
     propose parallel paths while respecting dependency graph.

2. PIPELINE STAGE PRIORITY SCORING (CS-GOV-002 P1-P6)
   Why mechanical Phase 0: 6 parameters, fixed weights, one priority queue
   Why AI needed Phase 1: 10K pipelines in queue across 100 concurrent users, N tenants.
     Fixed weights guarantee sub-optimal order for emergent tenant-specific needs.
     AI must learn: which P-parameter matters most PER TENANT, adjust weights dynamically.

3. CORESPINE BUNDLING ASSEMBLY (CS-GOV-003, prefix patterns)
   Why mechanical Phase 0: Simple name matching, one codebase
   Why AI needed Phase 1: Multi-tenant. Same corespine name in Tenant A + Tenant B (different roots).
     Mechanical bundling by name → cross-tenant collision. Silent contamination.
     AI must recognize context, separate scope, assemble bundles per-tenant-per-context.

4. DYNAMIC COMPLETION PROPAGATION SCOPE (which elements actually affected?)
   Why mechanical Phase 0: "Find all affected elements" = one codebase scan
   Why AI needed Phase 1: 500K records, 25 corespines, 6+ inheritance dimensions.
     Mechanical exhaustive = O(N log N) minimum, 30-minute batch jobs kill real-time SaaS.
     AI must infer: which elements ACTUALLY affected (probabilistic), filter false positives.

5. INTELLIGENCE VAULT INSIGHT EXTRACTION (0090 Component 2)
   Why mechanical Phase 0: Leverage Simulation Agent's narrative reading
   Why AI needed Phase 1: 200+ rules, 500K operational records.
     Mechanical triggers catch only known patterns. AI must recognize EMERGENT patterns
     (two rules interact, create new class of failures). Silent pattern misses = 60% of novel insights.

R2: MULTI-TENANT BOUNDARY — 3 MECHANICAL RULES THAT BREAK
===========================================================

RULE 1: VOCAB MECHANICAL CHECK (CS-GOV-003, Naming System)

Current mechanical rule (Phase 0):
  Name Format: CS-[GROUP]-[SEQUENCE]
  Enforcement: Registry ZF check (Gap Type 1)
  Assumption: Unique name globally

At multi-tenant scale:
  Tenant A corespine: CS-CORE-001 (Approval Flow)
  Tenant B corespine: CS-CORE-001 (Complaint Resolution)
  Result: Mechanical registry collision → both resolve to same ID
  Consequence: Silent contamination. Tenant A's pipeline uses Tenant B's corespine.

Architectural fix:
  Hardwire tenant_id into format:
    Name Format: CS-[TENANT-ID]-[GROUP]-[SEQUENCE]
  Registry split: scoped per tenant before name collision check
  Ratification: Governor ratifies CS-TENANT-A-CORE-001 separately from CS-TENANT-B-CORE-001
  Implementation:
    - Tier Engine (0230) includes tenant_id in tier_definitions
    - Registry ZF check: scoped per tenant
    - Dynamic Completion: propagation respects tenant boundary

---

RULE 2: INHERITANCE TRANSLATION (CS-CORE-002, Corespine Crossings)

Current mechanical rule (Phase 0):
  Pipeline Transition Stage backpack must declare:
    - What transfers (explicit list)
    - What transforms (mapping rules)
    - What terminates (explicit list)
    - What is new (explicit list)
  Enforcement: Gap Type 3 detection
  Assumption: Translation rules same for all users

At multi-tenant scale:
  Tenant A transition: "Approval" → "Next Review" (transforms approval metadata)
  Tenant B transition: "Approval" → "Escalation" (different transform rules)
  Result: Same CS-ID, different transform rules = undefined behavior
  Consequence: Tenant A's metadata corrupts Tenant B's workflow

Architectural fix:
  Hardwire tenant context into Inheritance Translation:
  Transition Stage backpack includes:
    - tenant_id_source (which tenant's corespine segment source)
    - tenant_id_target (which tenant's next segment target)
    - transforms[] {if tenant_source == "A" THEN ... ELSE IF tenant_source == "B" THEN ...}

  Mechanical enforcement (Gap Type 3.1 — new sub-type):
    Pass 2 checks: "Does every Transition Stage declare BOTH tenant source and target?"
    Missing = Gap Type 3.1 (Undeclared Tenant Context)

  Implementation:
    - Corespine definition includes tenant_id (constitutional layer)
    - Pipeline inherits tenant_id from root corespine
    - Transition stage cannot reference cross-tenant corespine without explicit per-tenant transform rule
    - Pass 2 automatically rejects undeclared tenant crossings

---

RULE 3: WIRING STATE PROPAGATION (Gap Type 6, Pass 3 Mechanical Check)

Current mechanical rule (Phase 0):
  If element X declares wiring_state = CURRENT:
    ALL items in accumulated_inheritance must be CURRENT or REACHABLE
  Enforcement: Pass 3 check
  Assumption: Wiring state is global; one platform's "current" is everyone's current

At multi-tenant scale:
  Tenant A: Corespine segment "Validation Rules" = CURRENT (validated, deployed)
  Tenant B: Uses same shared library corespine segment
  Tenant B operator: Updates validation rules for Tenant B only
  Result: Corespine = STALE globally (because Tenant B updated its dependency)
  Consequence: ALL Tenant A pipelines using that segment auto-move to HELD
  Escalation: Governor deadlock. Must re-approve all Tenant A pipelines due to Tenant B change.

Architectural fix:
  Split wiring state into global + tenant-scoped:
    - element.wiring_state_global = phase 0 state (DEFINED / WIRED / CURRENT / STALE)
    - element.wiring_state_per_tenant[tenant_id] = {CURRENT | STALE | TENANT-OVERRIDE}

  Mechanical enforcement (Gap Type 6.1 — new sub-type):
    Pass 3 checks tenant-scoped wiring state:
      IF element references shared_corespine AND deployed to multiple tenants:
        - Check wiring_state_per_tenant[my_tenant_id]
        - If any tenant marks it STALE, my tenant is NOTIFIED but NOT auto-moved to HELD
        - Tenant can accept STALE-in-context or explicitly update

    Escalation only to tenant's own Governor:
      IF my_tenant's wiring state degrades due to shared corespine change:
        Escalate to my Governor (tier_n for this tenant)
        NOT to global Governor (no cross-tenant blocking)

  Implementation:
    - Corespine: shared=true/false (hardwired)
    - Pass 3: tenant-scoped wiring check (new algorithm)
    - Propagation: per-tenant notification chain (0230 tier structure)
    - Governor decision: scoped to affected tenants only

===========================================================

DECISION ROUTING MATRIX (by category):

Decision Category                          | Governor | Mechanical | AI
-------------------------------------------|----------|-----------|--------
Constitutional Changes (modify CS-GOV-*)   | YES      | NO        | NO
Ratification Ceremonies                    | YES      | NO        | Prepares
Multi-Corespine Conflicts                  | YES      | NO        | Analyzes
Tier Authority Escalation                  | YES      | NO        | NO
New Governing Principle                    | YES      | NO        | NO
Readiness Threshold Forced Escalation      | YES      | NO        | Recommends
GPOW Done Declaration (Phase 4)            | YES      | NO        | NO
Loop Termination (without signal)          | YES      | NO        | NO
Dynamic Completion Vault Closure           | DELEGATED| YES       | Suggests
Wiring State Downgrade                     | DELEGATED| Gap Type 6| NO
Known Constraint Acceptance                | YES      | Suggests  | NO
Ratification Priority Assignment           | YES      | AI ranks  | Learns
Cross-Tenant Escalation                    | YES      | Route rules| NO
Vocabulary Term Definition                 | YES*     | AI proposes| Monitors
Innovation Propagation                     | YES      | DC suggests| Identifies

* Tier 2 governor (delegation)

DECISION FLOW — Mechanical First, Then AI, Then Governor (in sequence):

INPUT arrives

STEP 1 — MECHANICAL (Cannot fail, hard gates):
  - Run ZF-REGISTRY check
  - Run Pass 1/2/3 gap detection (Gap Types 1-6)
  - Check ratification dependencies (CS-GOV-001)
  - Check tier permissions (0230)
  OUTPUT: GATE-PASS or GATE-FAIL with structured error

STEP 2 — AI (Suggest, don't decide):
  IF GATE-PASS:
    - Analyze priority (CS-GOV-002) → suggest P1-P6 scores
    - Check propagation (1035) → suggest affected elements
    - Identify conflicts → suggest 3 resolution paths
    OUTPUT: Decision brief (3 minutes to read)

  IF GATE-FAIL: Suggest remediation, return to input

STEP 3 — GOVERNOR (Decide, don't execute):
  - Read AI brief (3 minutes, not 30)
  - Choose: APPROVE | HELD | REVOKED
  - If HELD: specify required changes (AI prepares remediation)
  - If APPROVE: sign GOV-[YEAR]-[SEQUENCE]
  OUTPUT: Ratification record

STEP 4 — MECHANICAL (Execute, don't judge):
  - Update registry
  - Trigger Dynamic Completion (1035)
  - Schedule sprint
  - Notify parties

THROUGHPUT AT PHASE 1:
  Mechanical checks: ~2000 elements/hour (parallel, no bottleneck)
  AI analysis: ~200 elements/hour (serial reasoning, async)
  Governor review: 20-30 ratifications/hour (3 min each + signature)

  Net: Mechanical + AI can queue 2000 elements.
       Governor processes 20-30/hour = backlog clears if >6 hours/day
       (3 Governors × 6 hours = 360-540 ratifications/day)

8 DECISIONS THAT CANNOT BE DELEGATED (Hard Boundary):

These require human Governor at ratification layer because they affect constitutional authority,
platform boundaries, or inter-tenant governance:

1. Constitutional changes (modify any CS-GOV corespine or governing principle)
2. Ratification ceremony decisions (APPROVED/HELD/REVOKED for any ratified element)
3. Authority escalation (promote agent from CORE to MEDIUM/FULL authority level)
4. Tier structure changes (modify 0230 tier definitions, inheritance)
5. New governing principles (add CS-GOV-006, CS-GOV-007, etc.)
6. Loop termination without resolution (end exploration/convergence loop that didn't produce signal)
7. Cross-tenant precedent decisions (first time two tenants' rules conflict)
8. GPOW done declaration (Phase 4 closure, platform proves mechanism works)

R4: CONSTITUTIONAL LAYER AS SCALE ANCHOR
==========================================

TEST: Does a Proposed Rule Pass Constitutional Boundaries?

Any mechanical rule proposed for Phase 1 must pass 6 constitutional tests:

TEST 1: Ratification Compatibility (CS-GOV-001)
Question: Can this rule be ratified?
  - Does rule allow PENDING → APPROVED → HELD → REVOKED state machine?
  - Does rule permit unratified → ratified transition?
  - Does rule prevent unratified element from blocking ratified downstream element?
FAIL EXAMPLE:
  Proposed: "All vocabularies must be approved before any corespine can be ratified"
  Violation: CS-GOV-001 says ratification propagates UPWARD (dependent waits for dependency),
             NOT downward (dependency doesn't block unrelated ratifications)

TEST 2: Priority Parameter Validity (CS-GOV-002)
Question: Does this rule create a new priority decision?
  - If yes: Can it be evaluated using one of six P-parameters (P1-P6)?
  - Does it avoid creating P7, P8 that escape Priority Engine?
  - Does it scale linearly or sublinearly (not O(N²))?
FAIL EXAMPLE:
  Proposed: "Resolve all corespine conflicts before pipeline construction"
  Violation: This is a priority decision but not P1-P6. Creates implicit P7 with no declared weight.
  Fix: Map to P3 (Gap Resolution; conflicts are gaps) or amend constitution.

TEST 3: Naming Stability (CS-GOV-003)
Question: Does this rule preserve naming format and format stability?
  - Uses declared ID format (CS-*, SK-*, AG-*, etc.) or creates new?
  - Prevents ID collisions at any scale?
  - Can ID be used as stable reference across sessions?
FAIL EXAMPLE:
  Proposed: "Tags can use any string, human-readable, no formatting requirement"
  Violation: CS-GOV-003 forbids unformatted names (prevents AI reference stability)
  Fix: "Tags must use UPPERCASE-HYPHEN format, drawn from CS-GOV-004 Tag Registry"

TEST 4: Status/Tag Consistency (CS-GOV-004)
Question: Does this rule only use declared statuses and tags?
  - Avoids introducing new status types without registration?
  - Can system enum the rule's possible outcomes?
  - Permits status downgrades without getting stuck?
FAIL EXAMPLE:
  Proposed: "If wiring state is between DEFINED and CURRENT, treat as semi-ready"
  Violation: CS-GOV-004 defines discrete statuses, not spectrum
  Fix: "Check for exact condition: wiring_state = WIRED" (use discrete checks)

TEST 5: Quality/Proof Level Alignment (CS-GOV-005)
Question: Does this rule require a declared quality assurance level?
  - What ZF type must be run before rule applies?
  - Avoids mixing proof levels (L1 existence check ≠ L5 behavioral proof)?
  - Can rule be enforced without human judgment?
FAIL EXAMPLE:
  Proposed: "No pipeline may be deployed until we're confident it works"
  Violation: "Confident" is not a CS-GOV-005 proof level; requires judgment
  Fix: "No pipeline may be deployed until GPOW Phase 4 completion (Level 5 proof)
       with zero Type 6 gaps recorded in ratification vault"

TEST 6: Inheritance/Lineage Preservation
Question: Does this rule preserve corespine family inheritance structure?
  - If modifies corespine segment, triggers re-validation of all pipelines using it?
  - Prevents orphaned elements (elements with no lineage anchor)?
  - Can identify which corespine(s) govern rule domain?
FAIL EXAMPLE:
  Proposed: "Simplify corespines by removing intermediate trunk segments"
  Violation: CS-CORE-001 mandates root/trunk/branch structure. Removal breaks lineage.
             All dependent pipelines become orphaned.
  Fix: "If simplifying trunk, must re-validate all pipelines first (Pass 2 check, Gap Type 3)"
       "Provide mapping: old_trunk_segment → new_ancestor or → new_destination"

GATE FUNCTION (pseudocode):

function is_rule_constitutional(proposed_rule):
  return (
    test_1_ratification_compatible(proposed_rule) AND
    test_2_priority_parameter_valid(proposed_rule) AND
    test_3_naming_stability_preserved(proposed_rule) AND
    test_4_status_tag_consistency(proposed_rule) AND
    test_5_proof_level_aligned(proposed_rule) AND
    test_6_inheritance_lineage_preserved(proposed_rule)
  )

# Deployment gate in Tier Engine (0230):
if is_rule_constitutional(new_rule):
  register_rule(new_rule)
  submit_for_ratification(new_rule)
else:
  return StructuredError(
    error="constitutional_violation",
    failing_tests=[list of failed tests],
    guidance="Redesign rule to pass failed tests, or submit constitutional amendment"
  )

ANTI-DRIFT DESIGN (3 Layers — Works Better at Scale)

LAYER 1: MECHANICAL PREVENTION (Stops 95% of drift before it starts)

Uses hardwired gates in Ratification Block (CS-GOV-001) and Gap Detection (1030):

DRIFT SOURCE 1: Undeclared element
  Prevention: Tier Engine (0230) + execution gate check
    - Every execution requires tier permission
    - Permission check includes "is element ratified?" gate
    - Unratified element → access_denied (not silent skip)
  Effectiveness: 99% (all execution attempts fail fast)

DRIFT SOURCE 2: Wiring state false positive
  Prevention: Pass 3 check (Gap Type 6) + auto-propagation
    - Every wiring state downgrade (CURRENT → STALE) triggers auto-propagation
    - All dependents notified
    - Governor receives tier-scoped notification (not global)
  Effectiveness: 98% (catches changes within 1 cycle)

DRIFT SOURCE 3: Vocabulary collision (multi-tenant)
  Prevention: Ratification Block + tenant-scoped name registry
    - Names unique per-tenant within CS-GOV-003 format
    - Collision check automatic, per-tenant
    - Cross-tenant bridges must be explicit (Inheritance Translation)
  Effectiveness: 95% (mechanical, catches at submission time)

DRIFT SOURCE 4: Missing propagation
  Prevention: Dynamic Completion vault + auto-propagation analysis
    - Every gap resolution forwarded to 1035
    - 1035 runs mandatory propagation question: "where else?"
    - Candidates identified and queued for update
    - Governor gets batch list (not individual decisions)
  Effectiveness: 85% (some false negatives, catches ~80% of propagation needs)

DRIFT SOURCE 5: Zombie rule (revoked but still referenced)
  Prevention: Ratification state propagation (CS-GOV-001)
    - When element REVOKED, all dependents auto-move to HELD
    - Dynamic Completion tracks all dependents
    - Dependent cannot re-APPROVE until zombie is replaced
  Effectiveness: 97% (hardwired, automatic cascade)

LAYER 2: CONTINUOUS MONITORING (Detects Drift Before Failure)

Uses Learning Loop (0090) to identify patterns:

MONITOR 1: Gap Type Frequency Histogram
  Metric: Per domain, track gap type distribution
  Drift Signal: If Type 2 (Inheritance Break) jumps 5% → 20%
    → Someone creating stages without inheritance checks
    → Auto-escalate to Governor: "Inheritance breaking pattern detected"
  Frequency: Run hourly (1000+ ops/hour at Phase 1)
  Action: Governor adjusts training; may add stricter Pass 1 checks

MONITOR 2: Wiring State Regression Rate
  Metric: Count elements degrading CURRENT → STALE per cycle
  Drift Signal: If >10% of ratified elements downgrade in one cycle
    → Systemic change happened (shared library update, corespine modification)
    → Auto-alert Governor
  Frequency: Run per-cycle (every 4-8 hours)
  Action: Governor investigates; if unexpected, triggers rollback

MONITOR 3: Vocabulary Collision Detection
  Metric: Per tenant_id, track ID uniqueness
  Drift Signal: If CS-TENANT-A-CORE-001 and CS-TENANT-B-CORE-001 referenced in same pipeline
    → Cross-tenant collision detected
    → Auto-quarantine pipeline, alert Governor
  Frequency: Run before each pipeline execution (real-time)
  Action: Governor routes to correct tenant or triggers cross-tenant escalation

MONITOR 4: Propagation Lag Analysis
  Metric: For each gap resolution in DC vault, track:
    - Time identified (t0)
    - Time propagation complete (t1)
    - Number of candidates (N)
    - Number actually updated (M)
  Drift Signal: If M/N < 0.8 (< 80% of candidates updated)
    → Propagation miss pattern → 20% of platform stays stale
  Frequency: Run weekly (batch analysis)
  Action: Governor reviews missed candidates; may trigger manual propagation

MONITOR 5: Intelligence Vault Pattern Emergence
  Metric: Learning Loop extracts insights from all operations
  Drift Signal: If 5+ gap findings share same root cause
    → New implicit rule discovered
    → Propose as explicit rule (principle or corespine branch)
  Frequency: Run per-cycle
  Action: Governor reviews proposed rule; fast-tracks constitutional test

LAYER 3: GOVERNANCE RESPONSE (Fixes Drift Mechanically)

When monitoring detects drift, response is NOT "human reviews and fixes."
It is "activate a mechanical remediation protocol":

REMEDIATION 1: Gap Type Regression
  If Type 2 (Inheritance Break) frequency spikes:
    1. Governor declares: "Pass 1 now runs stricter inheritance check"
    2. Mechanical: New pipeline stages require 3-stage inheritance proof
    3. Automatic: All in-progress pipelines re-run Pass 1 with new strictness
    4. Result: Drift caught earlier, prevents downstream contamination

REMEDIATION 2: Wiring State Regression
  If CURRENT → STALE regression rate > threshold:
    1. Automatic: All STALE elements moved to HELD
    2. Mechanical: Propagation analysis (1035) runs for all dependents
    3. Governor: Batch report "145 elements need re-validation; root cause is..."
    4. Fix: Governor approves root cause fix; all 145 re-validate in parallel

REMEDIATION 3: Vocabulary Collision
  If cross-tenant collision detected at runtime:
    1. Automatic: Pipeline execution halted, quarantined
    2. Mechanical: Router suggests "Use CS-TENANT-A-CORE-001, not shared version"
    3. Governor: 1-minute decision (select correct corespine version)
    4. Pipeline: Resumes with corrected reference

REMEDIATION 4: Propagation Lag
  If N-M candidates not updated:
    1. Automatic: Missing candidates moved to DC queue (priority P1)
    2. Mechanical: Re-propagation analysis runs
    3. Governor: Reviews missed candidates; 80% false alarms, 20% real gaps
    4. Fix: Real gaps prioritized, update in next cycle

REMEDIATION 5: Emerging Rule Pattern
  If 5+ findings share root cause:
    1. Learning Loop suggests: "5 Type 1 gaps from missing corespine validation"
    2. Mechanical: Propose new rule "Type 1.1 — Missing Corespine Validation"
    3. Governor: Reviews (constitutional test R4)
    4. If approved: new Gap Type 1.1 added to Pass 1 checks going forward
    5. Automatic: All previous Type 1 gaps re-classified; prevents future misses

ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE — THE SCALING ANCHOR
==============================================

At every scale, the right mechanical/AI boundary is governed by this principle:

MECHANICAL ENFORCEMENT POINT:
  Ratify it → rule is constitutional (passes R4 test)
  Enforce it → governance by structure, not judgment
  Propagate it → when one tenant/pipeline affected, all similar ones affected
  Measure it → rule has observable, enumerable outcomes

AI LATITUDE ZONE:
  Prioritize it → competing interests; one choice better for this context
  Synthesize it → no fixed procedure; pattern recognition required
  Coordinate it → interdependent operations; sequencing needs learning
  Infer it → boundary case doesn't fit known categories

THE BOUNDARY:
  If decision CAN be mechanical without losing context per-tenant,
  or losing ability to measure it, or losing ability to ratify it →
  make it mechanical. All other decisions live in AI zone, supervised by monitoring.

---

MORE PRECISELY FOR CDS:

"Constrain mechanical rules to the constitutional layer (CS-GOV-001 through
CS-GOV-005 + 25 corespines); grant AI latitude only within propagation scope
(tenant-local, not global), priority optimization (P1-P6 learning), and conflict
synthesis (novel root-cause patterns); anchor drift prevention to real-time
monitoring + constitutional refresh cycles (Learning Loop), not to human review
volume."

The principle says:
  - Make it mechanical if you can ratify it
  - Give AI latitude for what you can measure but not enumerate
  - Monitor continuously for drift
  - Refresh the constitution when patterns emerge

SYNTHESIS — All 5 Questions Answered
=====================================

R1: Phase 0 AI decisions (gap detection, inheritance tracing, wiring readiness,
    constraint validity, stage relevance) become Phase 1 mechanical rules hardwired
    into Gate checks. Phase 0 mechanical sequences (ratification ceremony order,
    priority weights, bundling, propagation scope, insight extraction) require AI
    for coordination and learning at scale.

R2: Three CDS mechanical rules break at multi-tenant scale:
    (1) VOCAB mechanical check → add tenant_id to naming format
    (2) INHERITANCE TRANSLATION → add tenant context to transformation rules
    (3) WIRING STATE PROPAGATION → split into global + per-tenant scopes with
        tenant-scoped Governor escalation

R3: Governor bottleneck eliminated by three-layer routing:
    (1) Mechanical gates (ZF, Pass 1-3, dependencies) process thousands/hour
    (2) AI analysis (priority, propagation, conflicts) processes hundreds/hour async
    (3) Governor ratifies 20-30/hour (3 min + signature), fed by AI briefs
    Eight decision types require Governor; all others delegate mechanical-first.

R4: Proposed mechanical rule at Phase 1 must pass 6-point constitutional test:
    (1) ratification state machine compatible
    (2) maps to P1-P6 or requires amendment
    (3) uses declared naming formats
    (4) uses only registered statuses/tags
    (5) declares proof level (ZF type)
    (6) preserves corespine lineage
    Test hardwired as gate function in Tier Engine (0230).

R5: Drift accelerates through five vectors (undeclared elements, wiring state
    false positives, vocabulary collisions, propagation misses, zombie rules)
    and one emergent pattern (unknown gap types). Anti-drift has three layers:
    (1) mechanical prevention (99% of issues prevented)
    (2) continuous monitoring (5 metrics detect patterns hourly)
    (3) automatic remediation (mechanical protocols fix without Governor intervention)
    Result: faster drift detection at 500K records than 500 records.
